# Reusable Deep-Research Prompts

Paste these into a research engine (Perplexity Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, ChatGPT Deep
Research, Elicit, Consensus…) whenever the briefing system needs another evidence pass. The
2026-07-11 run of both prompts is summarized in `briefing_psychology_research.md`.

---

## Prompt 1 — Upgrading daily / morning / weekly reflection briefings

> I maintain a personal life-OS app. Every day it generates three AI "briefings" from the user's
> journal entries, task sessions, health and finance data:
>
> 1. **Morning briefing** — momentum forecast + 3 directives phrased as implementation intentions
>    (currently uses: behavioral activation, implementation intentions, WOOP/mental contrasting,
>    identity-based habits, Self-Determination Theory, anti-perfectionism).
> 2. **End-of-day briefing** — honest summary, capability improvements, people to be grateful for,
>    gratitude list (currently uses: CBT distortion-spotting, emotional granularity,
>    self-compassion, deliberate practice, Stoic dichotomy of control, gratitude specificity).
> 3. **Weekly review** — GTD get-current/get-creative, Atomic Habits friction + identity votes,
>    improved abilities, gratitude highlights.
>
> Research question: **which additional intervention elements have the strongest experimental
> evidence for improving well-being, follow-through, or insight when embedded in short written
> daily/weekly reviews?** For each candidate element report: the canonical studies and effect
> sizes, the active ingredient, dose/format guidance (how many items, how often, wording), known
> backfire conditions (e.g. hedonic adaptation, forced positivity, rumination), and a concrete
> one-sentence spec of how it would appear as a JSON field in an AI-generated briefing. Candidates
> to evaluate at minimum: Three Good Things with causal attribution, savoring & anticipatory
> savoring, capitalization (sharing good news), expressed vs private gratitude, self-distancing
> (Kross), the progress principle / small wins (Amabile), after-action reviews, energy audits,
> peak-end effects in review design, best-possible-self, values affirmation. Add any element I
> missed with comparable evidence. Prioritize meta-analyses and RCTs; flag anything that is
> practitioner lore only.

## Prompt 2 — Designing a monthly briefing / monthly review

> Same app as above. I want to add a **monthly briefing**: a once-a-month AI-generated review that
> synthesizes ~30 days of journal entries, the month's four weekly reports, well-being scores
> across 12 areas (PERMA-like), task/project time data, finance and health summaries, and the
> user's people/relationship notes.
>
> Research questions:
> 1. What does the evidence say a **monthly-cadence** review should do that daily and weekly
>    reviews cannot? (temporal landmarks / fresh start effect, trend visibility, narrative
>    identity, goal re-calibration, relationship maintenance cycles…)
> 2. Which structured formats have evidence behind them: after-action review (gap analysis),
>    wheel-of-life / life-domain balance scans, best-possible-self projection, WOOP for the coming
>    month, stop-doing lists, gratitude reminiscence?
> 3. What are the failure modes of monthly reviews (too long, guilt-inducing, backward-looking
>    only, vanity metrics) and what design mitigations exist?
> 4. Deliver: a recommended section-by-section blueprint for the monthly briefing (section name,
>    what it contains, the evidence behind it, and cautions), ordered for psychological impact —
>    honest closure of the month first, then forward motivation.
> Prioritize meta-analyses, RCTs, and field experiments (e.g. Dai/Milkman/Riis on temporal
> landmarks; Keiser & Payne on AARs; Carrillo on BPS). Flag low-evidence sections explicitly.
